ADR 0179 — The loop asks Jev which tool, and the user's model sees only the menu that remains

Status​

Accepted. 2026-09-29 (AT-238, story HS-063, epic EP-029). Implements the AT-235 specification with the sixteen maintainer decisions of AT-236 (2026-09-28) and the 2026-09-29 decision on the adoption criterion, below. It replaces no ADR: it adds a piece outside the provider contract of ADR 0041 and the model catalog of ADR 0042, and reuses metering, the budget (ADR 0110) and the holder's credential (RN-058) without changing them.

Context​

Today whoever chooses the tool for each step of an agent is the user's chat model, looking at a catalog of 7 to 19 definitions on every call. The measurements of 2026-09-27 to 29 (docs/explanation/medicao-do-jev.md) put Jev — the typesafe/jev-1.13 model of OpenRouter's Decisions API, which answers a multiple-choice question with probabilities — against 328 real steps already recorded:

  • Jev top-1: 73% (66–79%), below the 90% AT-237 required for Jev to CHOOSE alone. Choosing and forcing does not work.
  • menu coverage of policy P3: 91% (85.1–94%) — the fraction of steps in which the tool the agent actually called is INSIDE the menu {Jev's choice, previous tool of the same run}. Jev's top-2 covers 90%. Coverage is the CEILING of end-to-end accuracy (that of the chat model, now over 1.6 tools instead of 7.4), not the accuracy.
  • P3 offers ONE single tool in 98 of the 160 validation steps, and the definitions the model reads per call drop from 746 to 138 tokens.

Jev, therefore, serves to restrict the menu, not to decide for it — and that is what AT-236 had already decided (answer 12: Jev only restricts).

The maintainer's decision on the criterion (2026-09-29)​

AT-237's adoption criterion was a confidence interval lower bound of 90%. No policy reached it (P3: 85.1%). On 2026-09-29 the maintainer lowered the criterion to 80%, with the numbers above in front of him, and ordered P3 to be implemented. This is the owner's decision, not a finding: P3's lower bound (85.1%) exceeds 80%, and no other text in this repository should say adoption "passed the 90%". What the measurement does NOT prove remains declared:

  • coverage is a ceiling, and the end-to-end accuracy measured in replay was between 62% and 91% depending on the hypothesis about the chat model;
  • the Wilson interval treats 160 steps as independent and they are 10 runs — the honest interval is wider;
  • the real validation is AT-239, live, comparing the turn's steps, latency and cost with and without Jev. This ADR delivers the mechanism, on by default as AT-236 ordered, with the per-workspace switch at hand.

Decision​

1. The router lives in the api, inside llm-turn, behind its own port. ToolRouter (apps/api/src/application/ports/tool-router.port.ts) is not an LLMProvider and is not in the LLMProviderRegistry: Jev decides, it does not converse, does not go through the provider contract suite and does not enter the models catalog. The adapter (JevToolRouter) talks to POST https://openrouter.ai/api/alpha/decisions, with the format MEASURED in scripts/jev/jev.ts — questions is an object keyed by the question id (the AT-235 note wrote a list, and the API answered 400). A single use case, DecidirFerramentaDoPassoUseCase, is called by BOTH turn use cases (RunLlmTurnUseCase and StreamLlmTurnUseCase) after the resolved provider and the budget gate pass and before provider.chat. Seven conversational agents, the ToolLoop of dev agents and gates, and the Infra Lead all go through them (AT-236 answer 2); only the Anamnese (paused) and the compaction summarizer stay out.

2. When it is consulted — all conditions, and only then is there a call: the turn model's provider is openrouter (AT-236 answer 1, reading (a)); the request carries two or more tools; there is an agent in the turn; the engine did not ask for the whole catalog; the workspace did not turn it off. Jev's key is the SAME one already decrypted for chat — the workspace holder's (RN-058) — and no new credential read is born. With provider ≠ openrouter no call goes out, not even the workspace read.

3. The policy is P3, and it only restricts. The step's menu is {tool chosen by Jev, previous tool of the same run}, in catalog order. If Jev answers responder_sem_ferramenta, or there is no previous tool in the step's catalog, the menu is the whole catalog (AT-236 answer 12: "whole catalog, only record"). There is no tool_choice: only the menu is restricted, the model can still answer in text (answer 7). There is no confidence threshold in v1 — the measured P3 does not use one, AT-236 answer 4 ordered it out of the measurement and P3's measurement does not produce one. Confidence is RECORDED on every step, and it is AT-239's job to propose a threshold (per agent, if the curves differ).

4. The state is what the product really has before the step — the fields of the escopo variant, the second round's winner, cut inside the api from the messages the engine already sends, with no database read: agente (the agentId), contexto (the beginning of the first system message, 1,500 characters), pedido (the last user message) and passos_recentes (the last 6 tool calls AFTER that message, argument and result cut at 500). Ceiling of 8 thousand tokens per request, a quarter of Jev's window; exceeded, fallback estado_grande. Two differences from what was measured, declared: contexto is the beginning of the real system, and not "actor identity + instruction" as in the replay; and in a dev agent under correction the pedido is the correction message, the last user, not the kickoff.

5. The turn NEVER fails because of Jev. HTTP error (includes the 404/410 of an alpha endpoint that changed), timeout (ceiling of 2,000 ms, TOOL_ROUTER_TIMEOUT_MS; 0 of 328 requests exceeded it in the measurement), network error, unexpected JSON, choice outside the options, large state, name collision with the reserved option: the provider receives the whole catalog and the reason goes to the event with RN-059's origin (infra for the first three, modelo for the two shape ones, codigo for ours). The adapter never throws, and the use case still protects itself with a last-resort try/catch.

6. Policy does not change. Jev does not approve, does not deny and does not choose a model. The tool remaining in the menu still becomes a Proposed Action and goes through decide() and the absolute ceilings as always; the router does not even import those modules, and an engine test asserts that its module does not know them. If the model calls a tool that was NOT on the step's menu (remembered from history), the engine dispatches it as today and the event only records foraDoCardapio (AT-236 answer 13: Jev narrows, does not forbid). The iteration ceiling stays intact: the Jev call is INSIDE the same llm-turn, and the loop only increments after dispatch.

7. Per-step record, for AT-239 to measure. The engine records tool_router.decided in the EngineApiClient facade (a single place, for the seven servers and the ToolLoop) with the menu before and after, choice, confidence, second option, previous tool, aplicado, motivoDaQueda, origemDaQueda, latency, cost and foraDoCardapio. Metrics come from a script over the log and over token_usage, never annotated. The api returns the step in the new optional field toolRouting of llm-turn and of the stream's final frame; an old engine ignores it.

8. Jev's spending is real spending. One token_usage row through the same RecordLlmUsageUseCase (the only metering path, with project, session and AREA budget): actor is the agent itself (answer 10 — with its own actor the spending would escape ADR 0110's area budget), provider = openrouter, modelId = null, modelName = typesafe/jev-1.13 (that is how the Spending tab separates Jev from chat, answer 8), estimated = false and costMicros = the RESPONSE's usage.cost, in micro-USD. The price per million is implicit (cost ÷ tokens of the response itself, answer 11) and the row says so in the new column price_implicit, keeping tokens × price = cost (RN-044) without pretending a catalog price exists. The budget gate is not re-checked between Jev and chat (answer 15). Jev's cost returns to the engine in toolRouting.custoMicros, and the ToolLoop adds it to its local budget without touching usage. Brabo's metering for CHAT records less than OpenRouter charges (catalog price, no cache nor reasoning); Jev's does not suffer from that, because it uses the cost the response returns.

9. The switch belongs to the WORKSPACE (answer 3), column workspaces.tool_router_enabled, default on, migration 0066, changed through PUT workspaces/:workspaceId/tool-router (owner, the owner of the key that pays). The api reads the flag from its own database in the turn path; the engine reads nothing and makes no extra HTTP. Off, no Jev call goes out and no event is born. The installation variable TOOL_ROUTER=jev|off that AT-235 proposed was NOT born: the maintainer's decision was per workspace. Only TOOL_ROUTER_TIMEOUT_MS was born.

10. The path for the agent to ask for the full catalog — AT-281 showed that 61% of steps would have a ONE-tool menu, and a wrong menu cannot become a dead end. The mechanism is the simplest one that does not open a new containment door: if the menu was restricted (aplicado) and the model answered without calling a tool (not even in text recoverable via ADR 0020) and without a provider error, the facade repeats the step once with catalogoCompleto: true, which makes the api skip Jev. The repetition asks for MORE tools, never one the agent did not have; the api trusts nothing from the engine beyond "don't restrict me"; the menu was never a security boundary. In the conversational agents' stream it only repeats if NOTHING was written to the person (a delivered delta cannot be undone). The repetition's cost is the two chat calls, both metered; the first one's toolRouting stays marked repetidoComCatalogoInteiro. Discarded: a request_full_toolset as a new tool (it is new tool surface in every agent, and the model with a one-tool menu has no reason to call it) and report_blocked as a signal (it ends the loop).

Consequences​

  • Each eligible step gains a serial call (Jev, then chat): +~0.2–0.4 s of latency (the measurement's recorded rows) and ~US$ 0.00005 per step. The gain — fewer definitions per call, and maybe fewer steps — is a hypothesis, and AT-239 measures it.
  • When Jev is wrong and the menu leaves the model without the right tool, the cost is one more round (item 10). The rate of that repetition is in the event (repetidoComCatalogoInteiro) and it is the metric that the menu is bad.
  • The endpoint is ALPHA. If it changes, the manual smoke (jev-tool-router.smoke.spec.ts, ceiling US$ 0.05, skipped without OPENROUTER_TEST_KEY) fails and, in production, every step falls back to the whole catalog with erro_http or resposta_invalida in the event — the per-workspace switch exists for the case of the fallback being too noisy.
  • A timeout on Brabo's side may have been charged by OpenRouter without us seeing the usage.cost: that spending does not enter token_usage. Declared, not measured.
  • The key is one per workspace installation, not per agent nor per project.

What this ADR does NOT do​

  • It does not measure the gain (AT-239) nor calibrate a threshold; there is no threshold in v1.
  • It shows nothing on screen. AT-236 (answer 3) wants the screen to show that the step was routed by Jev, with the confidence, and (answer 16) the strip to narrate "choosing tool". The data exists (the event and the stream's tool_routing_started frame, which the engine ignores as an unknown type); the screen and the strip are apps/web work, outside this lane.
  • It does not change the agent servers beyond what the facade already covers, does not route the Anamnese and does not put Jev in the model catalog.
  • It does not decide on raising the 2,000 ms ceiling nor on the Dependabot ecosystem for the typesafe/jev-1.13 pin (bump by PR, answer 5).
