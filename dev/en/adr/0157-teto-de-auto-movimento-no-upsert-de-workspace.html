ADR 0157 — The self-movement ceiling on the workspace upsert, and the self-promotion that was a loophole

Status​

Accepted. It is the THIRD in the line opened by ADR 0127 and continued by ADR 0156. It closes the last move the two left declared in writing, and revises one sentence of 0127: self-PROMOTION, which it recorded in the Consequences as a capability that stayed, is now refused. An accepted ADR is not edited — this one references it and says what changed. It corresponds to BRB-002, a security P1 of the maintainer's register.

Context​

The two previous ADRs closed the three doors of project_members — the two write ones and the removal one — and each of them pointed, in so many words, to what was left open. 0127:

downgrading the workspace owner in the workspace. Ceiling 1 is about project_members. POST workspaces/:workspaceId/members remains an upsert with no ceiling at all — same class of defect, scope above, not addressed here.

And 0156, reaffirming:

Extending the ceiling to the workspace. POST workspaces/:workspaceId/members is an upsert with no ceiling at all, as ADR 0127 already declared (…) Different scope, separate decision.

The defect, measured. AddWorkspaceMemberUseCase was a twelve-line pass-through: execute(workspaceId, userId, role) calling workspaces.addMember. It did not receive the actor, and the controller (@Post(':workspaceId/members'), @RequireRole('owner')) called it with the bare body, without @CurrentUser() — there was nothing with which to apply any ceiling, just as there was nothing on removal before 0156.

It is the same class of defect one scope ABOVE, and it is more serious for two reasons that do not exist in the project:

  1. there is no level above to hold the fall. In the project, downgrading oneself drops the effective role to the workspace role, which often holds it. In the workspace the role is the role: an owner who writes themselves as viewer loses the whole workspace, and with it all projects, the LLM spend that is theirs by RN-060, and the very ability to invite whoever would replace them;
  2. there is no member removal route to undo it by another path. WorkspacesController has no member @Delete — 0156 had already measured that and used it as an argument not to extend the ceiling in that PR. Undoing is THIS same route, which requires the freshly abandoned owner. There is no way back through the screen, nor through the api.

The second half: self-promotion. ADR 0127 wrote, in the list of what remained possible:

self-PROMOTION. A maintainer can write themselves as project owner. It was already possible before this ADR and still is; the ceilings are about going down, and opening the question of going up here would have changed, in passing, a capability nobody asked to change.

There was a test pinning this — "raising one's own role is not a downgrade and still passes" —, with a workspace maintainer writing themselves as project owner. The decision not to open the question in that PR was right by process, and the answer, now that it is open, is that it is a loophole. Both halves are the same move — a person deciding alone what authority they have —, and the upper one is the only one of the two that escalates privilege: 0127 could say its ceilings were not about escalation ("nobody gains a role they did not have"), and about self-promotion that is false. A maintainer becomes project owner without anyone promoting them, and in a workspace with sensitive projects it is the difference between restricting and being unrestrictable.

Decision​

1. The workspace upsert receives the actor and refuses self-movement. AddWorkspaceMemberUseCase.execute becomes (workspaceId, atorId, alvoId, papel) — the same order AddProjectMemberUseCase has had since 0127 and RemoveProjectMemberUseCase gained in 0156 —, the controller passes @CurrentUser(), and changing one's OWN role there is 403.

2. The ceiling does NOT count owners. Refusing only when the caller is the last owner would be the obvious "safe" rule, and it is rejected by the literal criterion of ADR 0127: "it is stated in a clause, it has no number to age". A count ages in three ways — it introduces one more read that can fall out of phase with the write; it has to decide what to do when the second owner is a service account or a person who left the company, which the api cannot tell apart; and it makes the route's answer depend on the state of OTHER rows, so the same call with the same body passes today and refuses tomorrow without anything the caller controls having changed. Whoever wants to leave their own role asks another owner, which is the same outcome ceiling 2 already produced in the project.

And there is an argument stronger than aesthetics: the clause already produces the invariant the count would exist to guarantee. A workspace never ends up without an owner, because the route requires owner and the only actor able to remove the last owner would be themselves — which is exactly the refused move. The number does not need to be counted because the shape of the rule makes it unreachable.

3. Ceiling 1 does NOT have a pair in this scope, and the absence is a decision. It was really considered, because here — unlike 0156's removal — the move EXISTS: an owner can downgrade another owner. The refusal comes from ceiling 1 being a rule about hierarchy inversion, and inversion not being possible in this scope:

  • in the project, projectRole ?? workspaceRole makes the project row OVERRIDE the workspace one (RN-471), so a maintainer reaches whoever is ABOVE them. That is how 0127 described the damage: "the owner loses their own project (…) restoring requires maintainer, which the owner just lost in that scope". Whoever acted was BELOW whoever suffered;
  • in the workspace that does not happen. @RequireRole('owner') is the top of ROLE_ORDER, so whoever calls the route is never below anyone it reaches. The precondition of ceiling 1 is absent — it is not a forgotten symmetry, it is a rule without a subject.

And the cost of adding the pair anyway would be high and irreversible: added to the ceiling of point 1 and the absence of a removal route, owner would become an absorbing state — nobody would ever leave it over HTTP, neither by their own will nor by another owner's hand. Offboarding someone who leaves the company, ownership rotation and correcting a wrong invitation would come to require a direct database write. That is the class of state ADR 0127 was born to eliminate, assembled again with the sign flipped.

Downgrading another owner, therefore, STAYS — and it is reversible through the same route, by any remaining owner, which the project move was not.

4. Self-promotion closes, on BOTH association routes. Ceiling 2 stops being "nobody downgrades themselves" and becomes "nobody touches their own role", in both directions, in the project and in the workspace. Rewriting the SAME role still passes: an idempotent upsert is not a move, and refusing it would break seed.ts, which rewrites roles on purpose.

5. The rule is not copied: the comparison becomes ONE classifier. The module domain/iam/tetos-de-rebaixamento.ts gains autoMovimentoDoProprioPapel, which returns the DIRECTION ('rebaixamento' | 'promocao' | null) instead of a boolean. It was this, and not a separate ehAutoPromocao, because the caller needs the direction: the decision of point 6 requires different messages, and two boolean predicates would make each use case ask the same comparison twice to find out which of the two answered. ehAutoRebaixamento still exists and becomes a reading of the classifier (=== 'rebaixamento'), byte for byte in behavior.

It survives for a behavioral reason and not a compatibility one: whoever delegates to it is remocaoEhAutoRebaixamento, and REMOVAL only sees the lower half. Widening it would make removal also refuse self-promotion — and removing one's own project row that restricted someone is precisely how one undoes the restriction ceiling 1 prevents from being created, the benign move point 3 of ADR 0156 protected explicitly. Unifying everything would have changed, in passing, a door this ADR did not open.

6. The messages are specific by DIRECTION and by SCOPE. There are four, and none serves two things: whoever tried to promote themselves does not receive the downgrade sentence (it is not what they did), and whoever hits the workspace ceiling is not sent to talk to a maintainer — there the role that undoes is owner. It is the same discipline as point 5 of ADR 0156 and of RN-470: do not collapse two states because they share an outcome.

7. The actor's role comes from the repository, not from ResolveEffectiveRoleUseCase. It is the inverse of 0156's choice, for the same reason it gave ("no extra dependency for one read"): in the project use cases ResolveEffectiveRoleUseCase enters so the actor's role does not become a second composition of projectRole ?? workspaceRole written by hand; in the workspace there is no composition to protect — forWorkspace IS workspaces.findMemberRole —, and this use case already has the repository in hand, because it is with it that it writes.

8. 403, in the use case, never in RolesGuard. Nothing new: the guard authorizes the CALLER against the route's @RequireRole and sees neither the body (dto.role) nor the target (dto.userId), and this ceiling is about the actor↔target relation. The whole reasoning is in ADR 0127 and is not repeated here.

9. Promotion is enforced even though it is unreachable over HTTP on this route. With @RequireRole('owner'), the actor is already at the top and there is nowhere to go up — the upper half of the ceiling, in the workspace scope, is a mechanism that does not fire today. It enters anyway, and tested, for the reason ceiling 2 always had not to have a threshold: the use case does not presume the guard, and it is what stays right the day the route changes its minimum. It is the same design as the capability refusal of RN-514, implemented and tested before having a trigger.

What this ADR explicitly rejects​

  • Counting owners. See point 2. Neither "only the last one", nor "only if no other active owner remains". The clause has no number to age, and already produces the invariant of never having zero owners.
  • A workspace owner ceiling in this scope. See point 3. It is neither a forgotten symmetry nor a reflexive discard: it is a rule about hierarchy inversion on a route where inversion is impossible, and adding it would make owner a state nobody leaves over HTTP.
  • A workspace member removal route. Its absence is part of this ADR's context (it is why self-downgrade has no way back), and creating it would be opening a new door while closing another — and the new door would be born needing the same ceiling, plus the judgment about removing the last owner, which this ADR does not have the material to make. A separate decision, if someone asks for it.
  • Widening the project member REMOVAL ceiling to promotion. See point 5. ADR 0156 protected that move in writing and it stays byte for byte.
  • Touching ResolveEffectiveRoleUseCase.forProject. Third ADR in a row in which this is said: projectRole ?? workspaceRole stays in both directions.
  • A gate on the screen. There is no screen: POST /workspaces/:workspaceId/members has no caller in apps/web — measured; the only occurrence is the generated type in api-types.generated.ts. Whoever calls the route today is seed.ts (through the use case, with the owner as actor) and direct integrations. There is no toast to adjust, and no web line changes.

Consequences​

A legitimate maintainer who needs owner in the project now depends on another person. It is the direct cost of the decision of point 4, and it is not hypothetical: it was the path through which someone with the route's role gave themselves the role they needed for the next task. It remains reachable — by any other maintainer of the project, and by any owner of the workspace —, and what is bought is that role elevation now takes two people. It is the same trade ADR 0127 made for going down, with the same price in the same currency.

ADR 0127's sentence about self-promotion becomes FALSE, and it is this ADR that replaces it. It remains written there, like every superseded decision in this repository; whoever reads it has to get here. That is why the index line and the docblock of ehAutoRebaixamento name the revision.

The test that pinned the permission was INVERTED, not deleted, with its name keeping the origin — "raising one's own role was allowed for being 'not a downgrade', and becomes 403 (ADR 0157)". The same form 0156 used for the removal gap test. It is the evidence of why the capability existed, and it is not thrown away.

One more query per workspace association, and only one: the actor's role. The route is an administration one and the comparison is pure, so the cost is the same 0127 accepted in the project (where there were two) and 0156 on removal (two).

The signature changed, and there were TWO callers — the controller and apps/api/src/db/seed.ts, which passes owner.id as the actor. There the actor and the target are different people on purpose, so no ceiling is reached and the seed gains no exception at all. CreateWorkspaceUseCase is not a caller, and it is good that it is not: it writes the creator as owner through the repository, inside the creation transaction, which is a legitimate self-promotion — the only one in the product — and would come to be refused if it were ever routed through here. The comment next to the null-role clause records this.

The route's surface changed without changing any success response, and it is in docs/security-surface.md: role:owner remains in the table and remains what RolesGuard enforces, but it is no longer the complete answer to "who can do what here" — the third route in this family to receive that note.

Still possible, by name: an owner downgrading ANOTHER owner (point 3, reversible through the same route); downgrading anyone who is not the caller, in any scope, which is the usual capability; and self-PROMOTION through the removal of one's own project row — removing the row that restricted someone raises their effective role, and ADR 0156 protected that move on purpose.
